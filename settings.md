# Settings

Timber Empires has two kinds of settings.

**Game settings** change how a game plays. They're picked when the game is created: on the last page of the New Game screens, click Customize, and they're at the end of the list under Timber Empires, after the game's own. Only the ones that apply to the mode you picked show. Your picks are remembered for the next game, and a new game takes them whether you open the list or not. They're saved with the game and can't change afterwards. In a multiplayer game the host's are everyone's.

**Your own settings** change what you see and hear. They're in Mod Settings, on the main menu and in the game.

BeaverBuddies adds two game settings of its own under Multiplayer: whether every player can use the dev tools, and the most players a game takes.

**Before you load or join.** The Load Game screen shows a save's game mode and the game settings that aren't their defaults, above its picture, and all of them when you hover. In the list of open matches, a match's details list the host's game settings. When a Quick match is found, the message says which settings differ from the defaults. Where the two players' difficulties or settings differ, a coin flip picks whose are played, and the search tries players with the same ones first.

## Science per player

Each player has their own science (in Co-op it's always shared). Districts earn it for their owner, and whoever unlocks something pays for it and gets it only for themselves. Districts nobody owns earn for a shared pool, which anyone can spend once their own points run out.

## Border protection

Nobody can demolish, pause or change the settings of buildings and beavers in someone else's districts.

## District specializations

Each district can pick a production focus. See [District specializations](playing/economy.md#district-specializations).

## Costly expansion

Builders lay roads (they cost nothing but a builder's time), and every District Center after your first is a real building: 100 logs, 120 planks, 60 gears, 30 metal blocks and 800 science. Off, roads and District Centers appear at once and for free, as in the base game.

## Dam pressure

Water pushes on dams, levees, floodgates and walls, and thin, tall or unbraced ones give way. See [Dam pressure](playing/rivers.md#dam-pressure).

## Victory

How a PvP game is won: kill all the beavers, destroy the District Centers, or build the wonder first. PvE and Co-op have their own goals. See [Victory](playing/victory.md).

## Bandit camps

Places bandit camps when a new game starts on a map that has none. See [Bandits](playing/bandits.md).

## Bandit raids
{: #bandit-raids}

<span id="first-raid"></span>The cycle the camps start raiding players in, from 1 to 20. All the way left is no raids. See [Raids](playing/bandits.md#raids).

## Fog of war

Off shows you everything. Fog shows only the land around your land claims, buildings, beavers and troops, and hides other players' buildings, beavers and cursors outside it. Black fog is the same, and land you haven't explored yet is black. See [Fog of war](playing/territory.md#fog-of-war).

## Private top bar

With no district selected, the top bar, the well-being panel and the population graphs count only your districts. If you own none, they count the whole settlement.

## AI opponents

How many AIs the game runs, up to three, and the difficulty of each. See [AI opponents](ai-opponents.md).

## Shared colony

Everyone builds one colony together, with the same goods and science. Off, each player starts their own. See [Horde](playing/horde.md).

## First undead night

The cycle the undead raccoons first climb out of the ruins: 1 to 5. See [The nights](playing/horde.md#the-nights).

## Nights between hordes
{: #nights-per-horde}

How many nights apart the hordes come, 1 to 7. The longer the wait, the more each sunrise brings. A badtide's first night always brings a super horde.

## Horde size

How many come out in each horde and in the super hordes: 25% to 300%.

## Day and night length

Longer days to get ready, longer nights to hold out: 1× to 5×.

## Zombie toughness

How hard zombies are to kill and how hard they hit: 50% to 200%.

## Night rewards

How much a survived night pays out: 25% to 200%.

## Horde growth

How much bigger the hordes get each cycle: +20% to +150%.

## Hordes adapt to you

Hordes get bigger when you handle them well and ease off when you take losses: 0% to 200%. At 0% they don't adapt.

The New Game difficulties (Easy, Normal and Hard) fill in First undead night, Zombie toughness, Night rewards, Horde growth and Hordes adapt to you. You can still edit them. Horde size doesn't follow the difficulty.

## Show district owner colors

Tints each District Center and its paths in its owner's color.

## Fog quality

How the fog is drawn: darkening only, static fog (redrawn when the camera moves or what you see changes), or moving fog. Darkening costs next to nothing.

## Undead raccoons drawn

The most undead raccoons drawn at once, among those the camera sees: 2,000, 4,000, 8,000 or 16,000. More makes a thicker horde and costs frame rate.

## Battle music

Plays battle music while your troops or beavers fight.

## Mute war horns

Silences the horn that sounds when something of yours comes under attack, and the one at the end of a game you lost.

## Licenses and credits

A button that opens the mod's license and credits. See [Credits](credits.md).
